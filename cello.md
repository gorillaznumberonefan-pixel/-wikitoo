# Cello
An instrument that usually has four strings and is in the violin family.

## Description
The violoncello, also more commonly known as the cello, is an instrument in the violin family. It is smaller than a **double bass**, commonly known as a bass, but bigger than a viola. The strings are usually tuned to C2, G2, D3, and A3, with C2 as the lowest, and A3 as the highest.

## Structure & Components
The cello is made up of a body, a neck, a **fingerboard**, a **pegbox**, **tuning pegs**, strings, **fine tuners**, the **endpin**, the **scroll**, the **bridge** and the **tailpiece**. The cello often has a bow, **rosin**, a case, and a cloth to go with it. It also has two F shaped holes called **f-holes**. It is mainly hollow, with the only three non-hollow parts being the neck, scroll, and bridge.

## How It Works
Rosin is applied to the bow. The bow is then dragged across the strings. This is called **col arco**, but mostly shows up on sheet music as “arco”. There are many bowing techniques, but when you pluck, it is called **pizzicato**, often showing up on sheet music as “pizz”.

## Tuning
The strings are often tuned to C in the second octave, G in the second octave, D in the third octave, and A in the third octave. The tuning pegs make large adjustments that get you roughly to the note. The fine tuners make smaller adjustments that get you to the exact note.

## Sound & Range
The cello makes a very beautiful, rich sound, and has about the range of a normal voice, but the notes can range from C2 to around E6 in concert, but can go all the way up to E7 in some cases.

## Playing Techniques
The standard way of playing is with the endpin out enough for when you stand up, the scroll meets your nose. It rests between your knees, and the top of the body rests on your chest. You sit on the front half of the chair, and sit upright. On your right hand, your thumb rests on the mouth of the **frog**, and your fingers are lightly curled on the frog of the bow. On your left hand, your thumb is on the back of the neck, and your second finger, the middle finger, is lined up with the thumb.

## Sizes
The most common sizes are one fourth, one half, three fourths, full sized, commonly written as four fourths, and five fourths, though some people may refer to five fourths as oversized.

## Materials
The cello is mainly made of wood or carbon fiber, but contains metal. The bow is made of wood and horse hair, though, it too could be made of carbon fiber. The rosin, which goes on the bow hair, is made from tree sap. The cloth rubs off built-up rosin on the strings.
## History
The cello was invented in the 16th century and was mainly the same, though the cello did shrink over time; it is now about 3cm skinnier. Things like rubber endpin tips wouldn’t be invented until the 19th century, and rock stops in the 20th century.
## Oldest Surviving Cellos
There aren’t many cellos left from the 16th century; there are some left. Among these are The "King" Andrea Amati Cello, The Andrea Amati Cello, The Brothers Amati Cello, and Gasparo da Salò Cellos.
## Makers & Luthiers
Some **luthiers** known to make cellos are Stradivari, Guarneri, Goffriller, and Amati.
## Composers & Repertoire
Well known composers for the cello include, but aren’t limited to, Bach, Beethoven, Chopin, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák, Brahms, and Vivaldi.
## Role in Ensembles
The cello is mainly the motor/bass line of the song, and it occasionally gets the Hauptstimme, also known as the main voice.
## Modern Uses
Modern uses of the cello are in orchestras, ensembles, solo performance, and bass lines.
## Maintenance
A cello is kept in a room temperature spot where it will stay upright and temperature won’t change. If needed, the strings are unwound and replaced. Loosen the bow for storage. If damaged, the tailpiece should be replaced. If damaged, the endpin should be replaced.
## Terminology
- The **Double Bass** is another instrument in the violin family and will be covered in another article
- A **Fingerboard**, fretted or not fretted, is a board where an instrument player puts their fingers to play notes.
- A **Pegbox** is where the pegs of an instrument go.
- **Tuning Pegs** are pegs that strings attach to and are tuned by on an instrument.
- **Fine Tuners**, found mostly on instruments in the violin family, are almost like tuning pegs, but they slightly adjust the string rather than big adjustments.
- An **Endpin** is a metal rod that sticks out from an instrument, usually a double bass or cello, that helps keep the instrument where it is supposed to be and elevated.
- A **Scroll** is found on top of the pegbox of an instrument and serves little to no purpose other than decoration.
- A **Tailpiece** is found on the bottom/farthest side of an instrument in the violin family and is where the other end of the string goes, and usually has fine tuners.
- **Rosin** is a material that aids in the production of sound on an instrument and goes onto the hair of a bow. It usually comes in rectangular prisms or short cylinders.
- **F-holes** are holes on an instrument in the violin family, and help make the sound louder.
- **Col Arco** is when a stringed instrument player uses a bow.
- **Pizzicato** is when a stringed instrument player plucks the strings.
- The **Frog** of a bow is found on the opposite side of the tip.
- A **Luthier** is an instrument maker who specializes in stringed instruments.
